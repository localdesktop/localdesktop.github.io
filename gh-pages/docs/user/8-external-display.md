---
title: External Display
---

Connect your phone to a monitor or TV (USB-C to HDMI, a USB-C dock, or a wireless display) while Local Desktop is open, and the Linux desktop moves to the big screen. The phone screen turns into a trackpad, like Samsung DeX.

![The Linux desktop on an external monitor](/img/external-display-monitor.jpg#boxed)

## Trackpad gestures

![The phone as a trackpad, with the rotate and keyboard buttons](/img/external-display-trackpad.png#boxed)

| Gesture | Does |
|---|---|
| Slide one finger | Move the cursor |
| Tap | Left click |
| Tap with two fingers | Right click |
| Tap with three fingers | Middle click |
| Slide two fingers | Scroll |
| Touch and hold, then slide | Drag (hold the left button) |
| Touch and hold, then lift | Right click |

The bear at the top of the trackpad shows which way is up.

## Buttons

- **Rotate** turns the phone's screen a quarter clockwise, trackpad and Android keyboard included, for when you lay the phone sideways while auto-rotate keeps it upright.
- **Keyboard** opens the Android keyboard on the phone and types into the desktop. It also opens by itself when a text field on the desktop takes focus, instead of the desktop's own touch keyboard.

![The Android keyboard typing into the desktop](/img/external-display-keyboard.png#boxed)

## In the background

Switch to another app and the desktop stays on the monitor. A mouse or keyboard used on the monitor keeps working there, if Android lets the mouse pointer onto the monitor. Come back to Local Desktop to use the trackpad again.

## Good to know

- Unplug the monitor and the desktop comes back to the phone screen.
- The desktop takes the monitor's resolution and scales its UI to the monitor's density.
- If Samsung DeX starts instead, choose **Screen mirroring** in the notification shade, or turn off **Auto start when HDMI is connected** in the DeX settings.
